<Chapter n="01" title="Brief and site">
  The ARC 253 studio, taught by Clyde Carpenter, asked for houses for the scientists who work at the Salk Institute.
  Each house had to share a common wall with its neighbor. The houses were then aggregated into a group of 24, with
  communal spaces for the residents and a strategy for setting the whole community on the site: a hilltop overlooking
  the ocean.
</Chapter>

<Chapter n="02" title="A house built around a shared wall">
  The unit is a two-story house. The ground floor holds the living room, kitchen and dining area around a central
  stair; the upper floor holds the bedrooms and bathrooms. The plans and elevations show the houses in pairs, joined by
  the shared wall the brief required, with the cuts for sections A–D marked through the plan.
</Chapter>

<Chapter n="04" title="Twenty-four homes on the slope">
  The 24 houses are aggregated into two wings of parallel rows that follow the contour lines down the hillside. A
  communal area sits where the wings meet, and the top of the site is given to the entry road, a parking court and
  planted islands. The plan is oriented to the laboratories inland and to the Pacific Ocean below.
</Chapter>

<Chapter n="05" title="Living on the slope">
  Section 1 cuts through both tiers of houses. The upper row sits at ground level; the lower row steps down the
  hillside below it, held by terraced retaining walls. The levels marked on the section are, from top to bottom: ground
  level, the living rooms of the top units, the finished floor of the community pavilion, the second and first floors of
  the bottom units, and the swimming pool.
</Chapter>

<Chapter n="06" title="Lighting strategy">
  An integrated lighting study of the house. The plans and Section A-A map which spaces are lit by daylight, which need
  ambient lighting and which need task lighting. Reflected ceiling plans then place the fixtures, and a schedule sets
  the luminaire type and the required illuminance for each space.
</Chapter>
