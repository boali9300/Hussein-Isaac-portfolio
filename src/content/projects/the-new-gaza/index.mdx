<Chapter n="01" title="Research question">
  In the ARC 759 studio, taught by Anne Filson and advised by Seda Kayim, students work with a team of faculty
  advisors, bringing disciplinary expertise and a critical position to complete, influential proposals relevant to
  the Commonwealth of Kentucky and beyond. My research asked how architecture can improve the lives of people who
  have been displaced by and suffered from war, specifically the people of Gaza. How can Gaza be reconstructed
  while embracing its values and history? And how can the architecture of that reconstruction reflect the
  resilience Palestinians have shown in the face of oppression?
</Chapter>

<Chapter n="04" title="Facilitation, not a master plan">
  The proposal is facilitation instead of top-down master planning. The project aims to facilitate by providing
  concepts, resources and tools, and by collaborating with the community, so that the community can adapt the
  reconstruction process and build programs as they see fit. The timeline runs from destruction through site
  work, recycling and sorting materials, building the project and facilitating future construction, toward a plan,
  about five years ahead, to reconstruct Gaza and develop temporary spaces. The proposed spaces are a congregation
  space, a playground, outdoor seating, a workshop and storage, organized around the mosque.
</Chapter>

<Chapter n="05" title="Design proposal">
  The new spaces wrap the existing mosque and its courtyard. A radial congregation space sits to the west, storage
  and the workshop to the east, and a playground, outdoor dining and a park line Omar Mukhtar Street. Circulation
  links each space to the street, the mosque courtyard and the others.
</Chapter>
