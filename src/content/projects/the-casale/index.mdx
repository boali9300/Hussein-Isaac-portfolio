<Chapter n="01" title="Brief and site">
  The ARC 355 studio, taught by Mark O'Bryan, started from an existing tourist site in Italy. Its owners already ran a
  cheese-making facility, a winery and a large farm, and wanted to grow the business with new programs and housing.
  The brief asked for a complex of housing for tourists, together with programs rooted in the culture of the town and
  tied to the existing estate.
</Chapter>

<Chapter n="02" title="A water axis through the complex">
  On the ground floor, the guest rooms cluster around a hexagonal fountain court. A water channel runs from the
  fountain down the site, past a planted garden, and ends at a large pool along the lower edge. Beside the axis stand
  the new public buildings: a dining room, a hall of long tables, and the winery, with its kitchen, wine making, barrel
  storage, wine tasting and lobby. The existing estate buildings sit to the east. On the upper floor, apartments with a
  bedroom, kitchen, dining and living room sit above the public buildings, around a planted roof terrace, and a long
  pergola shades the pool.
</Chapter>

<Chapter n="03" title="Sections">
  Sections A-A and B-B cut along and across the water axis. Both set the finish floor at 0'-0" and the second floor at
  +15'-6". Below grade, a sunken court with a pool reaches −11'-0", while shallow pools sit at ground level along the
  channel.
</Chapter>

<Chapter n="04" title="Circulation">
  The main route follows the water axis from the fountain court to the arrival point, and continues along the
  buildings and the pool. Secondary paths lead out to the guest rooms and the edges of the site. Private stairs between
  the public buildings lead up to the apartments.
</Chapter>

<Chapter n="05" title="Construction">
  The wall section runs from footing to roof. A stone outer wall, laid on Type S mortar over metal furring, encloses
  exterior gypsum sheathing, fiberglass insulation and a plywood interior finish. The roof combines local and
  conventional materials: asphalt over a layer of clay mixed with hay, on plywood and timber framing, with a concrete
  parapet at the edge. The ground floor is oak on a foam pad over a concrete slab, carried by a 6" concrete foundation
  wall on a 36" × 36" × 14" strip footing.
</Chapter>
